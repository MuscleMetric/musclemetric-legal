One training record

A workout tracker should still be useful after the workout ends.

Many people start with notes, a spreadsheet or a basic set counter. Those tools can record numbers, but the useful part is what happens next: finding the previous session, comparing an exercise, checking whether training frequency is consistent, and seeing whether a goal is actually moving.

MuscleMetric keeps planning, live workout logging, training history and progress analysis connected. A planned session becomes the workout you perform, the sets you complete become history, and that history feeds the progress views you use later.

Strength and hypertrophy training

Built for people who want more than a simple set counter.

For strength training and hypertrophy work, the important numbers are usually simple: what exercise you performed, how many sets and reps you completed, the weight used, and how that compares with previous sessions. What to look for

What makes a useful workout tracker app?

A good workout tracker should reduce friction during the session and increase clarity afterwards. For MuscleMetric, that means four things: the next workout is easy to understand, logging is fast enough to use every session, completed training stays available as history, and progress views remain tied to the actual workouts that produced them.

Fast during training

You should be able to record a set in seconds and get back to training rather than navigating through unnecessary screens.

Historical context

A number is more useful when you can see the session, exercise and previous performances around it.

Understandable progress

Training trends should be explainable through completed sessions, volume, frequency, exercise performance and goals rather than an unexplained score.
